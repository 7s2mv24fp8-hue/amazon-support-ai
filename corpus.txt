The old lighthouse stood at the edge of the cliff, its light sweeping
across the dark water every ten seconds. Sailors said it had guided
ships home for over a hundred years, through storms that swallowed
smaller boats whole.

Maya climbed the spiral stairs each evening to check the lamp. Her
grandmother had done the same, and her grandmother before that. It was
a quiet kind of work: wind the mechanism, trim the wick, polish the
glass until it caught the last light of the sun.

"The sea doesn't care how long you've kept it company," her grandmother
used to say. "It will still take what it wants. Our job is just to make
sure someone can find their way back."

One night, a storm rolled in faster than the forecast promised. Maya
watched the light sweep out over waves taller than the lighthouse
itself. Somewhere out there, a small fishing boat was fighting to find
the harbor mouth. She kept the lamp burning, adjusted the mirrors, and
waited.

By morning, the boat had made it in. The captain climbed the cliff path
just to shake her hand. He said the light had been the only steady
thing in a night that tried to take everything else. Maya just nodded.
It was, after all, the job: not to stop the storm, but to be the one
fixed point a person could steer toward.

Function: add two numbers.
def add(a, b):
    return a + b

Function: check if a number is prime.
def is_prime(n):
    if n < 2:
        return False
    for i in range(2, int(n ** 0.5) + 1):
        if n % i == 0:
            return False
    return True

Function: reverse a string.
def reverse_string(s):
    return s[::-1]
